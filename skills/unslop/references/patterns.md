# Patterns to revise

Use this catalog as a prompt for judgment, not automatic substitution. Preserve meaning, evidence, intended tone, project terms, direct quotations, code, and warranted uncertainty. Keep a term when it is an established project term or the most accurate wording. Factual reference material does not need opinions, first person, informality, or artificial variation.

## Content

1. **Puffery.** Cut phrases such as "pivotal moment," "testament to," "evolving landscape," "setting the stage for," and "indelible mark." State what happened.
2. **Name-dropping.** Do not list outlets without context. Name the relevant source and what it says.
3. **Superficial -ing phrases.** Replace "highlighting," "ensuring," "reflecting," "showcasing," and "fostering" with a concrete fact, source, or action.
4. **Promotional language.** Replace words such as "vibrant," "groundbreaking," "renowned," and "must-visit" with neutral descriptions.
5. **Vague attributions.** Replace "experts believe," "industry reports suggest," and "some critics argue" with a named source, or remove the claim.
6. **Formulaic challenges.** Replace "despite challenges, it continues to thrive" with the relevant facts.

## Language

7. **Abstract AI vocabulary.** Replace words such as "additionally," "crucial," "delve," "enhance," "interplay," "intricate," "pivotal," "showcase," "tapestry," "underscore," and abstract uses of "landscape" with plain language when it clarifies the point.
8. **Fancy forms of "is."** Replace "serves as," "stands as," "boasts," and "features" with "is" or "has" where that is accurate.
9. **"Not just X, but Y."** State the point directly.
10. **Rule of three.** Do not force ideas into groups of three. Use the natural number.
11. **Synonym cycling.** Pick one name for a thing and repeat it instead of cycling through near-synonyms.
12. **False ranges.** Do not use "from X to Y" unless X and Y form a meaningful scale.

## Style and communication artifacts

13. **Em dashes.** Avoid an em dash used as a loose connector. End the sentence or use a comma when that retains the intended tone. Do not substitute parentheses, an en dash, or a hyphen used as a dash.
14. **Colon overuse.** A colon is useful before a list or example, not as a mid-sentence connector. Rewrite "If you are coming from traditional automation: instead of registering event handlers, you describe conditions" as a complete sentence.
15. **Boldface overuse.** Do not bold every proper noun or acronym.
16. **Inline-header lists.** Replace a label that restates its line, such as "**Performance:** Performance improved." A bold lead-in is useful when it names the item and the following sentence adds information.
17. **Title-case headings.** Prefer sentence case.
18. **Decorative emojis.** Remove them from headings and bullets.
19. **Curly quotes.** Use the quotation style required by the project. Use straight quotes when no local convention says otherwise.
20. **Chatbot phrases.** Remove stock closers and openers such as "I hope this helps," "Of course," and "Found the smoking gun."
21. **Cutoff disclaimers.** Find a source or remove "while details are limited."
22. **Sycophancy.** Respond directly rather than praising the question or agreement.
23. **Filler phrases.** Replace "in order to" with "to" and "due to the fact that" with "because." Delete "it is important to note that" when it adds no meaning.
24. **Stacked hedges.** Reduce "could potentially possibly be argued" to the least hedge the evidence supports.
25. **Generic conclusions.** Replace "the future looks bright" with the specific plan, result, or uncertainty.

## Jargon and plain speech

26. **Abstract metaphor nouns.** Avoid terms such as "substrate," "wedge," "vector," "locus," "vantage," "nexus," "primitive," "harness," "surface," "bedrock," "scaffolding," "modality," "paradigm," "gold-plating," "ratchet," "evacuate," "endgame," "north star," and "flywheel" when a concrete term is clearer. For example, replace "substrate" with "base," "wedge in" with "add," "evacuate" with "move out," and "ratchet" with the real mechanism or "a limit that only tightens."
27. **Feelings instead of mechanisms.** Replace "the database stays close at hand" with the mechanism or result, such as "`.toSQL()` returns the exact string sent to the database." If a sentence could appear unchanged in another project's documentation, make it specific or cut it.
28. **Dense sentences.** Split a sentence when the reader must backtrack to parse it.
29. **Passive voice.** Prefer a named actor when it matters: "the compiler validates queries," not "queries are validated." Keep passive voice when the actor is unknown or irrelevant.
30. **Weak adverbs.** Replace "significantly improves" with a measured change. Cut an adverb that props up a weak verb.
31. **Fancy synonyms.** Prefer "use" to "utilize" or "leverage," "help" to "facilitate," "many" to "numerous," and "if" to "in the event that" when the plain word is equally precise.
