# Style layers

Use this reference when drafting or reviewing a substantial technical document.

## Document modes

- A tutorial teaches through visible progress. Say what the reader will make, give concrete steps, and show expected results.
- A how-to solves a specific task. Assume competence and keep background brief.
- Reference describes facts, options, limits, and errors. Mirror the thing it documents and avoid persuasion.
- An explanation answers a bounded why question. Include relevant decisions, constraints, and alternatives, and distinguish evidence from interpretation.

Source: [Diátaxis](https://diataxis.fr/), fetched 2026-07-18.

## Sentence rules

- Address the reader as "you" when giving instructions. Use present tense unless a future event matters.
- Use commands for procedures. Put the condition or warning before the guarded step.
- Keep one instruction per sentence. Split another sentence when two independent thoughts make it hard to parse.
- Prefer active voice when naming the actor clarifies behavior. Passive voice is appropriate when the actor is unknown or irrelevant.
- Keep modifiers next to what they modify. Make pronouns and conjunctions unambiguous.
- Break up dense noun strings, retain articles that disambiguate, and use one name for one thing.
- Prefer periods to overloaded punctuation. Avoid idioms, filler, invented metaphors, and fancy synonyms when plain language is clearer.

## Structure and review

- Use sentence-case headings. Use numbered lists for ordered work and bullets for parallel information.
- Use code formatting for code and literal commands. Use bold for UI labels when it helps the reader find them.
- Give links descriptive text. Do not use "click here."
- Keep explanations, instructions, reference, and opinion in the mode that suits them. Link to a separate section or document when that boundary helps.

For procedures, put the common case before exceptions. Do not pre-announce future work. Read an awkward sentence aloud, then rewrite it if it remains unclear. Use serial commas and say when a list is partial instead of ending it with "etc."

Source: [Google developer documentation style guide](https://developers.google.com/style), fetched 2026-07-18.

## Controlled and global English

Use one word for one action and keep its meaning stable. Put warnings before the step they guard. Retain articles and small connective words when they prevent a misreading. Keep modifiers such as "only" and "not" beside what they modify. Do not drop verbs from parallel clauses. Avoid slash constructions and idioms when a complete plain-language phrase is clearer.

Source: [ASD-STE100](https://asd-ste100.org/) Issue 9, 2025, and Kohl, *The Global English Style Guide* (SAS Press). The original guideline text was fetched from the Internet Archive and SAS sample chapter on 2026-07-18.

The style rules are aids, not a replacement for meaning. Preserve technical terms, direct quotations, code, and warranted uncertainty even when they do not fit a mechanical rule.
